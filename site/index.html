The problem

The suspicious email reaches your staff before your security team sees it.

Phishing is written for the person who has to act on it: the clerk who pays invoices, the employee whose password “expires today”, the assistant who handles the CEO’s requests. They have seconds to decide and no tools to check.

Accounts payable

“Overdue invoice: payment required today”

The supplier is real and so is the invoice number. The bank details are new, and the sending domain was registered this week.

Every employee

“Your password expires in 2 hours”

The logo and layout copy your IT portal exactly. The link leads to a look-alike domain that collects the password you type.

Executive assistants

“Are you at your desk? Need a favour”

It carries the CEO’s name but comes from a personal address, and it asks for gift-card codes before a meeting ends.

The cost of guessing

Every unanswered doubt ends in a click, a ticket or silence.

Without a quick way to check, people make one of three choices. None of them is good for you.

  1. They click.

    One password typed into a fake login page gives an attacker a working account inside your organisation.

  2. They open a ticket.

    Every “is this real?” request lands on the same few analysts, in the same queue as the alerts that need them.

  3. They stop asking.

    When a report gets no answer, people stop reporting, and the next suspicious email is handled by guesswork.

The service

One mailbox, independent checks, one plain answer.

Each forwarded email is examined by several independent checks at once. Their findings are weighed into a single risk score and explained in words anyone can act on.

Links and domains

Every link is checked for suspicious patterns and compared, subdomains included, against a list of known phishing domains. Optional WHOIS and DNS lookups show how old a domain is and who registered it.

Sender authenticity

SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, and their alignment with the From address, show whether the email really comes from the domain it claims.

Pressure and pretext

Local pattern analysis flags urgent subjects, pressure phrases and requests to verify an account, without calling any AI service.

AI review Optional

Add a model that reads the email the way a careful person would, looking for deception and impersonation. Choose OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral, Groq or TypeSafe, or run a local Ollama model on your own hardware.

Threat intelligence and attachments Optional

Check URLs, domains and IP addresses against VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB and AlienVault OTX, and scan attachments for known malware with ClamAV.

?

It never calls an email safe for lack of evidence.

If too many checks fail, or the evidence is too thin for a confident answer, the report says Could not confirm it is safe and tells the reader to verify the sender first. A check that could not run is never counted as a clean result.

The answer

Four possible verdicts, each with a next step.

The verdict is the first thing in the reply, so the reader knows what to do before they scroll. The reasons follow, and the full explanation is attached as a PDF.

Questions

Before you deploy.

Something not covered here? Ask us by email or on GitHub.

Does it block phishing before it reaches the inbox?

No. It analyses the emails your staff forward to it and does not sit in the delivery path. Keep your existing mail filter. This service covers what gets past it.

Do we need an AI provider?

No. Link, domain, sender-authentication and wording checks run locally without any model. AI review is an optional layer, and it can use a local Ollama model if you prefer not to send email content to a hosted provider.

Does email content leave our infrastructure?

Not when you host it yourself with the baseline configuration. If you enable a hosted AI provider or a threat-intelligence feed, that provider receives the data it needs to answer: the email text for AI review, and URLs, domains or IP addresses for the feeds. Each integration is off until you switch it on.

What happens to an email I forward to antiphishing@montimage.eu?

It is analysed by the instance of the service that Montimage runs, and the verdict is sent back to the address you forwarded from. Forwarding it shares the email with Montimage, so do not use this address for mail you are not allowed to share. For confidential mail, run the service on your own infrastructure. The privacy policy lists who receives what and how long it is kept.

What happens when the service is not sure?

It says so. If too many checks fail, or confidence is too low, the verdict reads “Could not confirm it is safe” and the reader is told to verify the sender. It will not return a clean result it cannot support.

Which languages are supported?

Forwarded emails are recognised, and reports are written, in English, French, Spanish, German, Italian and Dutch. The language is detected from the forwarded message, so staff receive the reply in their own language.

What do we need to run it?

A server or container host for the Python 3.12 service, a MongoDB database, and an IMAP/SMTP mailbox for staff to forward to. Redis is optional for caching. Run apc validate to check the configuration before you start.
